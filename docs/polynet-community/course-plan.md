# Polynet community: courses and knowledge plan

Status: planning. Nothing written to Polynet yet.
Where it goes: https://www.polynet.ai/c/polynet (production), as drafts through the Polynet MCP. Neil publishes.

## The idea

The Polynet community is the hub for Polynet and its front door for new builders.
Knowledge takes someone from "what is this?" to "my community is live, and its AI is on".

Funnel: video / social / Discover → join Polynet's community → Start here → build your own community → turn on its guide → bring your own AI.

## Rules for every lesson

- One idea per lesson. Short: aim for 300-700 words. 20,000 characters is the hard limit.
- Ends with **Do this now**: one step in Polynet. That is how progress means something.
- Video slot on every lesson. Neil adds the video link in the app (the MCP has no video field).
- No prices. Link to the one page that has them: https://www.polynet.ai/#pricing-title (Pricing section of the home page).
- Plain words, the product's own names (Home, Knowledge, Pods, the guide).
- Only what is true today. Anything not built is left out, not promised.

## 2. Build your community, A to Z

Purpose: from nothing to a live community with a link people can join. For builders.
Done means: their community exists, has a front door, channels with first posts, one course, one event, and its link is out.
Format: same house format as Start here (one-line promise, `##` sections, lists, callouts, "Do this now" last). One video per lesson, screen recording of the real screen.
Ground truth: checked against main on Nov 23 (exact labels below are the app's own words).
In Polynet (Nov 23, hidden draft, second course): 10 lessons in this order: Start your community, Your front door, Who can join, Channels and your first posts, Your first course, Events and calls, Get paid, Pods, Bring people in, Your members and your numbers. Payments moved after building (build first, charge later); no prices, pricing link only.

### 2.1 Start your community
- **Promise:** your community exists in about two minutes.
- Where: the community's name at the top → **Start a community** (or Discover → **Start a community**).
- Step 1: **Name**; the address (`polynet.ai/c/<address>`, **Change** to edit, 3–32 lowercase letters, numbers, dashes); **Add a picture** / **Add a banner** (optional).
- Step 2, "What you're offering": **Who it's for, in one line**; **What members get** (optional); **Price** Free or Paid; **List it in Discover**.
- Step 3: "<Name> is ready" → **Your invite link** → **Copy** → **Open community**.
- Callout (NOTE): everyone can run one free community; more than one needs the Agent plan.
- Callout (TIP): the "How it looks in Discover" card on the right shows what people will see as you type.
- **Do this now:** start your community. Don't share the link yet.

### 2.2 Your front door
- **Promise:** a page that makes the right people want in.
- Your address is your join page: what people see before they join.
- What's on it: name, one-liner, about, picture, banner, host, what's inside.
- Writing it: one-liner = who it's for and the result; about = what members get, how it works, who runs it.
- Edit any time: Settings → **General** (Logo, Banner, Name, Address, One-liner, About).
- **Listed in Discover** vs unlisted: listed needs a name, one-liner, picture and banner; unlisted means only people with your link find it. Either way, joining still goes through your rules.
- **Do this now:** write your one-liner and about, add a picture and banner, and decide listed or unlisted.

### 2.3 Who can join
- **Promise:** decide who gets in.
- Free communities: Members page → **Who can join** → **Change**: "Anyone can join" or "People ask to join, and you say yes or no".
- Asking first: requests show as "N people are waiting to join" → **Review** → **Approve** / **Decline**.
- Callout (TIP): start with "Ask first" while you're small, so you meet everyone.
- **Do this now:** pick open or ask first.

### 2.4 Get paid
- **Promise:** charge for membership, paid straight to you.
- Settings → **Plans and pricing** → **Payments** → **Connect Stripe** (pick your payout country).
- **Base price**: Free, Monthly or Annual. Until Stripe is connected, joining stays free.
- **Plans** (**Add a plan**): name, monthly price, what it grants, which pods it includes. **Stop selling** to retire one; members keep the price they joined at.
- The join button becomes "Join $X".
- Callout (NOTE): Polynet takes a small fee on paid memberships, plus Stripe's. No figures in the lesson: link to pricing, https://www.polynet.ai/#pricing-title (the Pricing section of the home page; Neil, Nov 23: "point to pricing page").
- **Do this now:** connect Stripe, or skip this lesson if you're staying free.

### 2.5 Channels and your first posts
- **Promise:** a place people want to talk in from day one.
- **Add channel**: name, and Access "All community members" or "Invite-only".
- Start small: General plus one or two. Up to 10 shared channels.
- Channel menu: **Channel settings** (name, description "What this channel is for"); reorder by dragging.
- Pin one post per channel (**Pin to channel**) to say what it's for.
- Callout (TIP): post three things before you invite anyone. An empty room keeps people quiet.
- **Do this now:** set up your channels and write three posts.

### 2.6 Your first course
- **Promise:** one course that gets a new member their first win.
- **New course** → name it, **Add a line about it**, **Add a cover**.
- **Add a lesson**: title, a video (YouTube, Loom, Vimeo or Wistia), the words, then **Add a link** / **Add a file** (12 resources per lesson, files up to 100 MB).
- Writing well: headings, short lists, one callout for the key idea, a "Do this now" at the end. (This course is the example.)
- A course is a draft ("only hosts see it") until you switch **Published** on; it needs a lesson first.
- Drag to order lessons and courses. **View as a member** (under your name) shows what members see.
- Hosts see who finished what; members are told.
- Callout (TIP): your AI (Claude or ChatGPT) can write the course for you; see **Use your own AI**.
- **Do this now:** make one course with one lesson, and publish it.

### 2.7 Events and calls
- **Promise:** a rhythm people can count on.
- **Add an event**: Name, Starts/Ends, Time zone, **Repeats** (Once, Daily, Weekly, Monthly), **Where** (Online, A place, To be decided) and the **Link to join**, **What to know**.
- Members get a reminder email the day before, can **Add to calendar**, and join from Events when it starts.
- Switch on "On <community>'s public page" to show it on your join page.
- Callout (TIP): one weekly call beats five one-offs. This community's Friday call is the example.
- **Do this now:** put your first call on the calendar.

### 2.8 Pods
- **Promise:** smaller groups inside your community, free or paid.
- A pod is a community inside the community, with its own room, calls and gate.
- **New pod**: name, what it's for (one sentence), **Listed** (members can see it and ask to join), **Seats** (up to 500, or no limit).
- Getting in: free listed pods → "Ask to join" and you **Add** or **Decline**; paid pods → a price on top of membership ("Join · $X", needs Stripe); full pods → "Join the waitlist"; plans can include pods.
- **Schedule a call** inside a pod (every week, every month, or once).
- Callout (NOTE): most communities don't need a pod on day one. Add one when a group wants more.
- **Do this now:** decide whether you need a pod yet. If yes, make one.

### 2.9 Bring people in
- **Promise:** your first ten members.
- Your link: `polynet.ai/c/<address>`. **Invite people** (bottom of the left column) copies it.
- Where it goes: your YouTube descriptions, your bio, your posts, a DM to people who already follow you.
- Discover helps if you're listed, but your own audience comes first.
- Welcome each new person by name in #General.
- **Do this now:** put your link in one place your people already see, and send it to five people.

### 2.10 Your members and your numbers
- **Promise:** know who's here and what's working.
- **Members**: Member, Status, Plan, Joined, Last active; search; **Status** filter; export.
- A member's record: **Message**, notes only hosts see, payments; **Change role, remove or block** (Member, Moderator, Admin; **Remove**, **Ban**).
- **Analytics**: Members, Monthly revenue, Active, Join rate; a chart of new members, revenue or visits to the join page; 7, 30 or 90 days.
- What to watch first: join rate (is the front door working?) and active (are people coming back?).
- Callout (ACTION): what's next → turn on the guide (course **Your community's guide**).
- **Do this now:** open Analytics and note your join rate and active members.

- Lessons: what members see, and what the guide answers from.
- Sources: links and files you add in Knowledge; the guide learns from them, members don't see them.

### 3.4 What it does by itself
- Briefs: what's happening, on Home.
- Suggested replies to questions nobody answered, for you to post, edit or skip.

- Do this now: check Home tomorrow.

### 3.5 What always needs your Yes
- Role changes, removing or banning, removing others' posts, publishing the handbook, changing its instructions, opening the community, publishing or deleting a published course.
- Posts in your name: it asks first unless you switch that on.
- Do this now: look at Settings > Guide.

### Lesson 1: What Polynet is

Video: Neil's (set with `video` once recorded). Link: "Connect your own AI" → https://www.polynet.ai/ai. House format (Nov 23): one-line promise, `##` sections, a list, callouts, action block last. In Polynet since Nov 23.

```markdown
Polynet is a place to build a community that can think, remember and act. This lesson covers why that matters now, what Polynet does, and who it's for.

## The problem

Most online communities are three things: a chat, some courses and a calendar. That worked when a community's value was access: to people, to information, to the host.

AI changes that. Information is everywhere, and many services are turning into software. What stays hard to copy is a group of people who trust each other and are working on the same thing.

> [!IMPORTANT] The shift
> A community that only talks can't keep up. The ones that win will do things for their members: answer, remember, follow up and act.

## What Polynet does

- **It talks.** Channels, threads, direct messages, events and calls.
- **It remembers.** Courses and lessons in Knowledge, plus what's said in the open channels. What the community knows builds up instead of scrolling away.
- **It acts.** Every community can have its own AI guide. It answers from what the community knows, spots questions nobody answered, tells you what's happening on Home and helps the host run the place. You can also connect your own AI, like Claude or ChatGPT, to work in Polynet as you.

## Who it's for

Anyone who gathers people around something they know: coaches, creators, course builders, clubs, masterminds, and companies with customers. If people come to you to learn, get help or build together, it's for you.

> [!NOTE] What it costs
> Building a community is free. The AI is the paid part.

## See it working

This community runs on Polynet. Everything these courses teach, you can try right here.

> [!ACTION] Do this now
> Open the next lesson, **A tour**, to see where everything is.
```

Here's where everything is. On a computer it's all in the left column. On a phone there are three tabs at the bottom: Home, the community, and Inbox.

### Lesson 3: Meet the guide

```markdown
Every community on Polynet can have its own AI guide. In this community it's called Polynet.

**Two places to talk to it.**
- **In a channel.** Mention @Polynet in a post or reply and it answers in the thread, where everyone can see.
- **In private.** The box on Home ("Ask your guide anything") is your own talk with it. Nobody else sees it. You can also open it from Search or ask, or from Your guide under your name.

**What it knows.** This community's lessons, what's been said in the open channels, the events coming up and who's here. When an answer comes from a lesson, it says which one, so you can go to the source. If it doesn't know, it says so.

**It remembers you.** In your private talk it keeps short notes about you, like what you're building and what you want help with, so you don't have to repeat yourself. Ask "what do you remember about me?" to see them.

**It can do things for you.** Ask it to catch you up, find a post, draft your intro, react to something or mark a lesson done. Before anything goes out under your name, it shows you the words and waits for your Yes.

**What it won't do.** Touch money, share what's said in private places, or post for you without asking.

Good first questions:
- "What did I miss?"
- "Who should I meet here?"
- "What can I build with Polynet?"

**Do this now:** ask the guide one of those on Home.
```
